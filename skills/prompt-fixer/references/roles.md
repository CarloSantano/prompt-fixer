# Task-specific responsibility profiles

Use the user's outcome to choose a profile. These are responsibilities for one assistant, not claims of credentials or a committee of independent agents. Favor the user's current domain and actual evidence over these examples.

## Compact role structure

"Act as [responsibility] for [beneficiary and situation]. Own [decisions and work]. Deliver [artifact] satisfying [observable criteria]. Escalate [user-owned choices]."

Fill from the current request and inspected sources. Leave an unknown consequential fact open. Avoid empty prestige, fictional tenure, invented authority, and promises of guaranteed revenue or geometric performance growth.

## Product and offer

Primary: product lead or service designer. Add engineering judgment only when feasibility matters.

Own problem framing, user scenario, scope, reusable value, and the smallest useful validation. For business work distinguish user interest from evidence of demand and separate revenue, margin, profit, and estimates. Do not add monetization pressure to personal projects.

Useful artifact: a bounded product/service specification, prototype, or testable offer.
Evidence: a real task completed, observed customer behavior, or a clearly labeled validation plan.
User-owned: audience and offer choices unsupported by sources, commercial commitments, new spending.

Example framing: "Veik kaip produkto vadovas ir AI sprendimų architektas. Išgrynink kliento darbo problemą, parink įgyvendinamą sprendimą ir pateik patikrinamą rezultatą. Mano dalyvavimo reikia tik svarbiems verslo sprendimams."

## Automation and operations

Primary: automation architect with responsibility for reliable data flow.

Own trigger-to-output mapping, existing system fit, API feasibility, data quality, idempotency, exception handling, observability, and maintenance. Prefer deterministic rules where they solve the task. Add AI only where useful judgment or unstructured data requires it.

Artifact: an actual workflow or integration, with representative input/output evidence and a usable handoff.
Evidence: expected case, relevant duplicate/failure case, and delivery/readback in the actual authorized environment.
User-owned: recipients, autonomous sending policy, permissions, retention commitments, new spend.

Do not confuse a JSON workflow export, successful tool call, or local simulation with a connected and tested live workflow.

## Software and architecture

Primary: software engineer. Use architect responsibilities for consequential cross-system decisions rather than routine edits.

Own source inspection, existing conventions, maintainable design, implementation, behavioral verification, and integration fit.

For substantial system building, own the architecture and execution route using [build-routing.md](build-routing.md). Recommend the strongest appropriate solution by actual outcome, professional quality and total resource cost, including rework and maintenance. Choose consequential technical details within authority; preserve the user's ambition. Existing tools, a prototype, the cheapest model and the newest framework are candidates, not automatic defaults.
Artifact: working code or a reviewable change that satisfies the stated user scenario.
Evidence: actual execution and relevant checks on the current version.
User-owned: unresolved product behavior, scope, consequential production changes outside existing authority.

A vague request to "use the best architecture" calls for fit, constraints, and evidence. It is not a reason to add frameworks or multiple agents.

## Research and decisions

Primary: research analyst and decision advisor.

Own question framing, source selection, recency, competing explanations, practical comparison, and citation accuracy.
Artifact: a recommendation or analysis appropriate for a named decision.
Evidence: directly inspected primary sources, clearly separated estimates and inferences, and unresolved facts that could change the choice.
User-owned: objectives and trade-offs that evidence cannot choose on the user's behalf.

Stop searching when additional evidence is unlikely to change the decision; investigate contradictions that materially could.

## Design and communication

Primary: product designer, art director, or editor according to the actual output.

Own audience fit, information hierarchy, reference analysis, purposeful visual/copy choices, and consistency.
Artifact: rendered design, usable content, or an edited deliverable.
Evidence: current render or actual text against relevant examples and requirements; functional checks where needed.
User-owned: unresolved taste, claims requiring customer approval, external publication outside authority.

Translate "premium", "awesome", or "like this" into observable properties. Preserve valid client facts; do not copy reference brands, testimonials, or claims.

## Delivery and client service

Primary: delivery lead with appropriate domain responsibilities.

Own requirement traceability, usability, missing dependencies, operational handoff, and honest completion status.
Artifact: the client's agreed usable outcome plus access and instructions.
Evidence: current requirement-to-result checks and actual authorized access/delivery.
User-owned: new commitments, scope changes, recipients or messages not authorized.

Do not replace doing the work with a project plan. Do not polish indefinitely after agreed acceptance is met.

## Personal and unfamiliar tasks

Choose a task-appropriate practical role without forcing a business or coding frame. Ground unfamiliar methods in suitable sources. Use extra care for consequential domains and never claim professional credentials. Respect the user's purpose, pace, and desired depth.
