# Resource use and a real finish

Use when a task risks repeated research, large context, broad code generation, model/tool spending or endless polish. The objective is a checked useful result per total resource, including the user's attention, rework and maintenance. Preserve ambition and the agreed quality bar.

## Spend context where it changes work

Reuse current accepted decisions, inspected facts and working assets. Do not repeat intake or re-read unchanged material. Retrieve targeted originals or sections; load specialist references for actual gaps, not all available methods. Keep critical constraints and source provenance when compressing context.

Use deterministic search, transforms, schema validation and relevant tests where they fit. A second model call, subagent or independent review needs a concrete uncertainty to resolve and appropriate authority. Do not add a permanent multi-model loop to every message.

Choose stronger available tools or models when they improve expected task success enough to justify total cost. Lower token count alone is not a reason to drop requirements, architecture or critical checks. Do not claim actual token, price or credit savings without telemetry. Characters and words are explicitly labeled proxies.

When preparing a prompt, avoid generic obligations already supplied by the host or skill unless portability requires them. Preserve task-specific interfaces and constraints even when the prompt must stay longer.

## Keep commercial work practical

For a new commercial idea, focus on a specific customer job, evidence of demand, delivery feasibility, acquisition path and actual cost when those change the decision. Separate revenue, margin, profit and forecasts. Do not promise winning products or income.

For an agreed client project, prioritize delivery against the brief and actual acceptance. Do not restart market validation or add a new strategy engagement. For a personal app, prioritize the person's useful outcome and actual operating needs; do not impose monetization.

## Completion contract

Before costly work, identify:
1. The agreed deliverable and boundaries.
2. The user behavior or outcome that must work.
3. The smallest meaningful set of evidence and checks.
4. The remaining dependency or user-owned decision.
5. The stopping condition.

Keep this compact and reuse an existing sufficient contract. Do not make every task generate a planning document.

Iterate against a named failure or unmet criterion, not an abstract desire to improve a score. Check the affected behavior after a change; repeat broader checks only when a change, failure or unresolved risk warrants it. Diagnose repeated failures before spending more on the same approach.

When acceptance is met, deliver and stop. Park optional polish or new features with their trade-off. When blocked, name the exact gap and continue independent authorized work; do not silently lower the target or label a prototype as delivered production.

## Meaningful improvement

For repeated workflows, preserve a baseline and representative cases. Check both the rewritten prompt and a bounded downstream artifact against the original requirements. Keep grading criteria separate from the agent producing responses. Record resource measurements honestly, one-run variability and untested hosts.

A successful example does not establish universal behavior. Regressions, ties and cases needing human judgment are results to retain, not reasons to keep tweaking until the report looks better.
